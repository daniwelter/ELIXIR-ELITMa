In most ELIXIR Nodes, the majority of activities take place in projects. The <a href="https://www.pmi.org/about/what-is-a-project">Project Management Institute</a> (PMI) defines a project as a "**TEMPORARY** endeavor undertaken to create a **unique product, service or result**". Projects can be small, limited to one or two organisations, or large, involving many organisations in the same Node or even internationally. They can vary in duration from just a few weeks or months to multiple years. Projects also normally have a financial aspect, where funding is provided for the work to take place, so work needs to be monitored to ensure that the funds are spent correctly.

Many Nodes and organisations already employ dedicated project management professionals to ensure that project management activities are treated at as first class citizens rather than an afterthought and to allow researchers, developers and other project participants to focus on delivering the project content. This introduction is not intended for project managers but rather for individuals with little to no experience in project management.

## The project life cycle

Projects are split into several distinct phases. The number of phases and their exact definitions vary between different project management methodologies but generally include the phases shown in this figure:

<img src="{{ '/images/project-management/project-lifecycle.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Illustration of the project cycle with four sequential phases labelled 'Initiating', 'Planning', 'Executing' and 'Closing', and a separate 'Monitor & Control' phase running alongside. A dashed arrow from the end back to the beginning indicates cycling projects." class="img-fluid my-3">

Each of these phases are associated with a series of specific project management tasks:
- **Initiation**: Define the desired outcomes. Create a Business Case. Define the project scope. Get the project off to a good start.
- **Planning**: Assign the Project Core Team (PCT). Elaborate the project scope. Plan the work.
- **Execution**: Coordinate the exection of the projectp lans. Produce deliverables.
- **Close**: Coordinate the formal acceptance of the project. Report on project performance. Capture "Lessons Learned" and post-project recommendations. Close the project administratively.

In addition, throughout the project life cycle, project management involves tasks to **Monitor & Control** the progress of the project: Oversee all project work and management activities over the duration of the project; monitor peroject performance; measure progress; make changes; address risks and issues; identify corrective actions. 

### Linear vs evolving projects

Some project management metholodogies treat the project life cycle as a strictly linear process. These methodologies are best suited to well-defined projects such as large grant-funded projects, where all the project phases, including expected milestones and deliverables, are fully defined in advance. Examples of these methodologies include [PMP](https://www.pmi.org/certifications/project-management-pmp),[PRINCE2](https://www.prince2.com/uk/what-is-prince2) and [PM2](https://pm2alliance.eu/why-pm2/).

This model does not suit all types of projects, so other methodologies offer a more adaptive approach, where a larger project is broken down into multiple smaller projects, each with its own life cycle phases. This approach involves regular reassessment of objectives and progress, and allows for the adaptation and reprioritisation of at the beginning of each cycle. Examples of these methodologies include [SCRUM](https://www.scrum.org/learning-series/what-is-scrum/), [PMI-ACP](https://www.pmi.org/certifications/agile-acp), [PRINCE2 AGILE](https://www.prince2.com/uk/training/prince2-agile) and [PM2 AGILE](https://pm2.europa.eu/pm2-methodologies/pm2-agile_en).

## ELIXIR PM tools

A wide range of opensource and commercial project management tools exist, varying in features, methodology support and complexity, project management need not be complicated. The ELIXIR Hub uses a relatively simple spreadsheet format to manage, coordinate and monitor most of its projects. Table 1 provides an overview of the different tabs in the template and what they are used for:

| Tab	| Description	| Relevant for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dashboard		| Quantitative metrics ( Deliverables / Milestones) - Deliverables and Milestones submitted in the last month and due in the next 12 months with links to prefilled templates		| ALL  |
| Full View		| Quantitative metrics ( Deliverables / Milestones) - Deliverables and Milestones submitted in the last month and due until the end of the project with links to prefilled templates		| ALL |
| Commitments		| Project commitments mainly deliverables and milestones but others can also be added (e.g. GA, Review meetings.....)		| PMO |
| Parameters		| Information used to customize the reports according to the project		| PMO |
| UoR Baseline		| Current allocations of resources to project participants as described in GA 		| PMO |
| UoR Monitoring		| Utilisation of resources (PMs and €) quarterly report to be compiled by project participants		| Admin & Financial teams. PIs, PMO |
| UoR Deployment		| Specific for GDI, monitoring of the deployment of resources (FTEs) according to the targeted level		| PIs, Management Board, PMO |
| Change Management		| Modification to the GA (amendments) including dates and links to decisions		| PIs, Admin & Financial teams, Management Board, PMO |
| UoR New Baseline		| Impact of the modifications proposed o the GA in PMs and Budget ( UoR Baseline. + Change Management) =>  to be  incorporated into the  GA after the amendment is approved		| PIs, Admin & Financial teams, Management Board, PMO |
| Risk register		| Risk register for quarterly monitoring (Relevant risk)		| Management Board, PMO |
| Issues log		| Relevant Issues log		| Management Board, PMO |
| Contacts & DL		| Participant registered contact and distribution lists		| ALL |
